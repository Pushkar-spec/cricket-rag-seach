Cricket is a bat-and-ball game played between two teams of eleven players each on a field with a 22-yard pitch at its center. The game originated in England during the 16th century and has since become one of the most popular sports in the world, especially in countries like India, Australia, England, Pakistan, South Africa, and the West Indies.

The objective of cricket is for one team to score more runs than the opposing team. A run is scored when a batsman hits the ball and runs between the wickets, or when the ball crosses the boundary. Hitting the ball over the boundary without it touching the ground earns six runs, while a boundary hit along the ground earns four runs.

There are three main formats of cricket: Test cricket, One Day International (ODI), and Twenty20 (T20). Test cricket is the longest format, played over five days, and is considered the ultimate test of a player's skill and endurance. ODI cricket is limited to 50 overs per side and usually finishes in a single day. T20 cricket is the shortest format, with each team batting for only 20 overs, making it fast-paced and popular among fans for its entertainment value.

The International Cricket Council, commonly known as the ICC, is the global governing body for cricket. It organizes major tournaments such as the Cricket World Cup, the T20 World Cup, and the World Test Championship. The Cricket World Cup is held every four years and is one of the most watched sporting events globally.

Sachin Tendulkar, often referred to as the "God of Cricket," is widely regarded as one of the greatest batsmen in the history of the sport. He holds the record for the most runs scored in both Test and ODI cricket, and was the first player to score a double century in an ODI match. Other legendary players include Sir Don Bradman, who had an extraordinary Test batting average of 99.94, Virat Kohli, known for his aggressive batting style and numerous centuries, and Sir Vivian Richards, celebrated for his dominance during the 1970s and 1980s.

The Indian Premier League, known as the IPL, is a professional T20 cricket league in India and one of the most-watched sporting leagues in the world. It was founded in 2008 and features franchise teams representing different Indian cities, with players from around the globe competing for various city-based teams.

In cricket, the term "wicket" has multiple meanings. It can refer to the set of three stumps and two bails that the bowler aims to hit, or it can describe a batsman being dismissed. A bowler who takes three wickets in three consecutive deliveries achieves what is known as a "hat-trick," a rare and celebrated feat in the sport.

India won the Cricket World Cup in 1983, 2011, and 2025, while Australia holds the record for the most World Cup titles overall. The 2011 World Cup was particularly special as it was played on home soil, with the final held at the Wankhede Stadium in Mumbai, where India defeated Sri Lanka to lift the trophy.

Cricket bats are traditionally made from willow wood, chosen for its lightweight yet durable properties. The ball used in cricket is made of cork covered with leather, and its condition changes throughout a match, affecting how bowlers can use it to swing or spin.
